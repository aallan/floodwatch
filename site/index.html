About

Floodwatch shows which of the River Taw's gauges are high and rising, which storm overflows are spilling, what flood and weather warnings are in force, where the power is off, and what the rain and the river are forecast to do next, across the Taw's catchment in north Devon, from Dartmoor to Barnstaple. All of it is public data, published by the Environment Agency, South West Water, the Met Office and others on their own sites; here it's on one map, and most of it is checked every five minutes. Those sites keep only the latest weeks, or the latest spill, so Floodwatch keeps its own copy of every river and rain reading since January 2026, and every storm overflow spill since June.

On the map

Flood warnings
The Environment Agency's flood warnings and alerts in force in the Taw catchment, outlined on the map, with a banner across the top of the page while any are in force, and a tag in the header saying so while none are.
Weather warnings
The Met Office's yellow, amber and red warnings of severe weather that name Devon, in a banner of the warning's colour while any are in force. Each one opens to show the Met Office's own description of it, and links to it on the Met Office site.
Drought
The Environment Agency's drought status for its Devon, Cornwall and Isles of Scilly area from its weekly report, and South West Water's hosepipe ban, in a tag in the header that opens to show each stage as the report words it, with a link to the report.
Power cuts
National Grid Electricity Distribution's power cuts in the catchment, each with the customers off, the postcodes affected and when power is expected back on. To report a power cut, call 105.
Rivers and rain
The Environment Agency's river level, tidal and rain gauges in the catchment, and the rain gauges just over its watershed, reporting every 15 minutes, each with its latest reading on it. River gauges are teal within their typical range, amber near the top of it and red above it, with an arrow showing whether the level is rising, falling or steady, and grey once they've sent nothing for three hours. Gauges close together are grouped until you zoom in. Each gauge's chart goes back to January 2026, and every reading it has can be downloaded as a CSV file. Rain gauges have a 48-hour rain forecast, and the gauge at Umberleigh a fortnight's river flow forecast for the Taw, scaled to start from the flow measured there.
Storm overflows
South West Water's storm overflows in the catchment, which release storm sewage into the rivers and the estuary when heavy rain overloads the sewers. The map opens on those spilling now; those that spilled in the last 48 hours, and the rest once zoomed in, can be switched on from the legend. Each one's popup shows its spills over the last 30 days and its yearly totals.
Background
The catchment's rivers, in Floodwatch's colours, and its streams, with every stream that has a gauge on it always shown; the Tarka and Dartmoor lines into Exeter; and the A377 and the A30 west of Exeter, from OpenStreetMap. Rivers end where they meet the estuary rather than running down the middle of it, and the Taw runs on to Barnstaple's Long Bridge. Dartmoor and Exmoor national parks are shaded pale green.
